What does your HbA1c actually mean?

Your surgery gives you a number in mmol/mol. Half the internet talks in percentages. Here's both, plus what the result sits alongside.

HbA1c converter

Enter the number from your last blood test and switch between the two units the NHS and the internet use.

Typical UK reference points: below 42 mmol/mol (6.0%) is the normal range, 42–47 mmol/mol (6.0–6.4%) is the prediabetes range, and 48 mmol/mol (6.5%) or above is the diabetes range.

About

Hi, I'm Ellis ♥

EllisRN · NHS Diabetes Lead

If you've ever been told "you need to lose weight," "eat better," or "get your blood sugars down" — but were left wondering "Okay… but how do I actually do that?", you're in the right place.

I'm Ellis, a Registered Nurse and NHS Diabetes Lead, with a passion for helping people feel more confident and in control of their Type 2 diabetes and prediabetes.

Through my work in diabetes care, I've seen how overwhelming it can feel. There's so much information out there — what you should eat, what you shouldn't eat, how much exercise you need, medications, blood glucose numbers, HbA1c… and sometimes it can feel like you're expected to figure it all out yourself.

I believe diabetes education should feel different.

My approach is practical, realistic and completely without judgement. I'm not here to give you another strict diet or tell you that you need to completely change your life overnight. I'm here to help you understand what's happening in your body, make sense of the information you're given and turn that knowledge into small, realistic changes that fit around your life.

Because managing diabetes isn't about being perfect. It's about finding an approach you can actually live with long term.

Why I started The Diabetes Lifestyle Coach

My professional experience in diabetes care, combined with seeing the impact diabetes can have on people and families, is what inspired me to create The Diabetes Lifestyle Coach.

I wanted to create a space where people could get clear, evidence-based education and personalised lifestyle support without feeling judged, overwhelmed or like they had failed.

Whether you've recently been diagnosed, have been living with Type 2 diabetes for years, have been told you're at risk, or simply feel like you need some extra support — you don't have to figure it all out alone.

How I work

Three things I promise you

No judgement, just support

You won't be judged for where you're starting from — whether that's your weight, your blood glucose, food choices or simply feeling like you've lost your way. My job is to listen, understand what's going on and help you move forward without shame or guilt.

Evidence over internet noise

There's a lot of conflicting diabetes advice online. I'll help you separate fact from fad, using evidence-based information and my experience working in NHS diabetes care. No quick fixes, no miracle cures, no unnecessary restriction — and no cutting out entire food groups because the internet said so. Just clear, practical information you can actually understand and use.

A plan that fits your life

You don't need a perfect routine. Your work, family, budget, social life and everything in between matter. We'll focus on realistic changes that work for you — because a plan only works if it's something you can maintain. Small steps. Real life. Lasting change.

Important

What this is — and what it isn't

Diabetes coaching is there to complement your usual health care, not replace it.

I don't prescribe medication, change your prescribed treatment or replace your GP or diabetes team. Instead I give you the time, education and practical support to better understand your diabetes and the lifestyle factors that can influence it.

We'll discuss your lifestyle and the ways you can change it to make a real impact on your health and your diabetes.

If something comes up that needs a clinical assessment or medication review, I'll encourage you to speak to your diabetes team or GP.

Think of me as that extra layer of support — helping you understand, prepare and take action.

Common questions

Before you book

How much does it cost, and which package do I need?

The Diabetes MOT is £120 for one appointment, the 4 Week GLP-1 Success Package is £275, the 6 Week Refresh and Reset is £350, and the 12 Week 1:1 Transformation Programme is £600. If you're not sure which fits, that's exactly what the free 15 minute discovery call is for — I'd rather point you to the right one than sell you the biggest one.

What happens on the free discovery call?

Fifteen minutes, no pressure and no pitch. You tell me where you're at, ask me anything you like, and I'll give you an honest answer about whether I can help and which package makes sense. If the answer is that you don't need me, I'll say that too.

Do I need a referral from my GP?

No. You can book directly. Coaching sits alongside your NHS care rather than replacing it, so there's no referral and no waiting list — though I'd always encourage you to keep your diabetes team in the loop.

Will you change my medication?

No — I don't prescribe and I won't tell you to stop or alter anything you've been prescribed. What I will do is make sure you understand what you're taking and why, so that any conversation you have with your prescriber is a much better one.

I'm on a GLP-1. Can you help with that?

Yes. GLP-1 support is one of the things I'm asked about most — what to expect when starting, how to eat well while your appetite is suppressed, protecting muscle, and building the habits that mean the results hold.

What if I have Type 1 diabetes?

My coaching is built around Type 2 diabetes and prediabetes. If you have Type 1 and you're not sure whether I'm the right fit, message me and I'll give you an honest answer rather than book you in regardless.

Where do the consultations take place?

Online, by video, wherever you are in the UK. You don't need special software or a good camera — just somewhere quiet with a decent connection and 90 minutes where you won't be interrupted.

What happens after the consultation?

You get a written summary with your four-week action plan, practical next steps and relapse-prevention strategies. It's yours to keep, and yours to act on with or without more support from me.
