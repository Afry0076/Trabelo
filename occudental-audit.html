It's clear you're a highly regarded practice — the awards, the specialist oral surgeon on-site, and the range of treatments set you apart from most dental practices in Sheffield. But your performance score is 53 out of 100, and your main content takes 21 seconds to load on mobile. That's a gap between the quality of care you offer and the first impression your website makes.

Your Best Practices score is a perfect 100 — that's worth noting. But performance, accessibility, and SEO all have room to improve, and I've put together a free demo showing exactly what could be done about it.

What's Going Wrong

Critical
21 seconds to load on mobile — patients aren't waiting that long
Largest Contentful Paint of 21.0s on mobile. Your First Contentful Paint is 6.1 seconds — so patients stare at an empty screen for over 6 seconds before anything appears.
Why it matters to you

Someone searching "private dentist Sheffield" or "emergency dentist S10" on their phone is making a quick decision. If your site takes 6 seconds just to show something — and 21 seconds to fully load — most of them will have moved on to a competitor before they've seen your name, let alone your awards or your specialist surgeon. For a practice of Occudental's calibre, that's a real missed opportunity.

What the fix looks like

A rebuild removes the render-blocking code (which is adding nearly 5 seconds of delay before anything even starts loading), compresses images properly, and eliminates the 809KB of unused JavaScript currently being loaded. The target is 97+ on performance — pages loading in under 2.5 seconds.

Critical
Render-blocking code adding nearly 5 seconds before the page starts loading
Estimated savings of 4,670ms from removing render-blocking requests — that's the single biggest drag on your load time.
Why it matters to you

Your site loads a large amount of CSS and JavaScript before it shows anything to the visitor. It's entirely avoidable — it's a consequence of the platform and template your site is built on, not something inherent to having a rich, well-designed website.

What the fix looks like

A hand-coded rebuild loads visual content first and defers everything else. This alone typically cuts 3–5 seconds from the time to first meaningful content.

Critical
809KB of JavaScript that isn't being used — loaded on every visit
Google flagged 809 KB of unused JavaScript and 251 KB of unused CSS — over 1MB of dead code being sent to every visitor.
Why it matters to you

This is the largest unused JavaScript figure I've seen on any Sheffield dental practice I've audited. It's a hallmark of sites built on large platforms that load their entire codebase regardless of what the page actually needs. Every visitor — on every page — is downloading over a megabyte of code that does nothing for them.

What the fix looks like

A custom-built site only ships the code it actually uses. No platform bloat, no unused libraries. What the page needs is what gets loaded — nothing more.

Critical
Total page size of 3.4MB is far too heavy
Google flagged an enormous network payload of 3,388 KB — a well-optimised site should be under 1MB.
Why it matters to you

Every time someone visits your homepage, their phone downloads 3.4MB of data. On a slow 4G connection — which is what Google uses to test — that takes a long time. Patients on limited data plans are being penalised just for trying to book with you.

What the fix looks like

A well-built site comes in under 500KB total. That's a 6–7x reduction — achieved through image compression, removing unused code, and efficient caching.

Critical
7 long main-thread tasks blocking the browser
Google found 7 long tasks on the main thread — more than any other Sheffield practice I've audited. This contributes directly to the 190ms Total Blocking Time.
Why it matters to you

Long main-thread tasks mean the browser is stuck processing JavaScript and can't respond to the patient's taps or scrolls. Even once the page appears to have loaded, it can feel unresponsive — a frustrating experience that reflects poorly on the practice.

What the fix looks like

Removing unused JavaScript and rebuilding without a heavy platform eliminates these tasks. A lean, hand-coded site has no long main-thread tasks at all.

Moderate
Images missing alt text — hurting both accessibility and SEO
Google flagged this under both accessibility (score: 85) and SEO (score: 85) — images without alt attributes can't be read by screen readers or indexed by Google.
Why it matters to you

Alt text is the description attached to each image. Without it, Google can't understand what your images show — so they contribute nothing to your search rankings. It also means visually impaired patients using screen readers get no information from your images at all. Given that you're actively promoting neurodiversity friendliness, this is particularly worth addressing.

What the fix looks like

Every image on a rebuilt site has a proper descriptive alt tag — both for SEO and for accessibility. This alone closes the gap between your current SEO score of 85 and a target of 100.

Moderate
Links without descriptive text — confusing for patients and Google
SEO score of 85 — one link found without descriptive text, plus links that are identical but lead to different places.
Why it matters to you

Google uses link text to understand what's on the page it's being sent to. Vague links like "Find out more" or identical links pointing to different places confuse both Google's crawler and patients using assistive technology. For a practice targeting high-value search terms like "private dentist Sheffield", every SEO signal matters.

What the fix looks like

Every link on a rebuilt site is clearly and uniquely descriptive. Combined with fixing the alt text, this brings the SEO score from 85 to 100.
