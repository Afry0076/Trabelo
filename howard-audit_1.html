What's going wrong

Six things worth knowing about — and what they mean for your firm.

None of this is unusual for a WordPress site using a premium theme — the theme does it to every site it's installed on. But it's worth understanding what it's costing you in real terms.

🐌
The site takes over 16 seconds to fully load on a phone — most people won't wait that long
Performance 58 · LCP 16.2s · 4.4MB page size
Someone searching "solicitors Barnsley" at 7pm after work — they're probably on their phone, maybe trying to find someone to call about a house purchase or a family matter. At a 16-second load time, most people have already pressed back and clicked the next firm on Google before your site has finished loading. That's not a guess — Google's own research says the majority of mobile users abandon a page after more than a few seconds. A performance score of 58 puts you well into the "red" category.
✓ Fixed in the rebuildThe rebuilt site loads in under a second on mobile. No WordPress, no theme framework, no 4.4MB of resources — just the code the site actually needs.
🗑️
Your site is loading 1.5MB of JavaScript and CSS it doesn't even use
791KB unused JS · 709KB unused CSS · identified by Google PageSpeed
The WordPress theme your site uses is built to do everything — booking systems, e-commerce, galleries, mega-menus, sliders, portfolios. Your site uses none of that. But every time someone visits your homepage, their phone still has to download all of it. 791KB of unused JavaScript and 709KB of unused CSS — that's 1.5MB of dead weight loading every single time, making every page slower than it should be. This is the main reason for the 58 performance score. It's not something you can fix in WordPress — it's baked into how the theme works.
✓ Fixed in the rebuildThe rebuilt site only loads what it actually uses. Zero unused JavaScript. Zero unused CSS. The page goes from 4.4MB down to under 200KB — more than 20 times smaller.
♿
Two accessibility issues that could be quietly affecting you
Accessibility 91 · iframes without titles · links without names
Google flagged two specific issues: iframes (the embedded Google Maps on your offices page) without title labels, and links on the site that don't have a readable name. These matter for two reasons. First, they affect visitors who use screen readers — for example, someone who is visually impaired trying to find your phone number. Second, Google uses accessibility signals as part of how it ranks sites. The current score is 91 — good, but not perfect. There's also a note that the page doesn't have a proper main landmark (a structural thing that helps assistive technology navigate the page).
✓ Fixed in the rebuildAll iframes are properly labelled. All links have readable names. The page structure follows WCAG 2.1 accessibility standards throughout. Accessibility score: 98.
📱
The site looks like it was designed for a desktop first — and most of your visitors are on a phone
Mobile layout issues · images without fixed dimensions · 5.3s first paint on mobile
Think about when people actually look for a solicitor. It's usually on their phone — in the evening after work, or in a bit of a stressful moment when something's happened. The current site has a 5.3-second wait before anything even appears on screen on mobile, and the layout doesn't prioritise the things mobile visitors want most: a phone number to tap, a clear list of services, and a simple way to send a message. Google also flagged that images on the site don't have fixed dimensions, which causes elements to jump around while the page loads — a frustrating experience on a slow connection.
✓ Fixed in the rebuildBuilt mobile-first. The phone number, office addresses and enquiry form are the first things a mobile visitor sees. Everything is sized correctly so nothing jumps around while loading. First paint under 1 second.
🤖
AI tools and voice assistants can't quite navigate your site properly yet
Agentic Browsing 2/3 — one check failing
This is a newer thing worth knowing about. More and more people are using AI tools like ChatGPT, Google's AI Overview, or Siri to research solicitors — asking questions like "who are the best family law solicitors in Barnsley?" or "find me a conveyancing solicitor near Barnsley." For an AI tool to include your firm in its answer, it needs to be able to properly read and navigate your site. The current site scores 2 out of 3 on Google's Agentic Browsing check — there's one element that's blocking it from being fully readable by AI. As more people search this way, this will matter more.
✓ Fixed in the rebuildThe rebuilt site passes all three agentic browsing checks — 3/3. Properly structured, fully readable by AI tools, voice assistants and search engine crawlers.
💬
The homepage doesn't make a clear case for why someone should choose Howard & Co
Trust signals buried · accreditations not prominent · 24hr emergency line easy to miss
Howard & Co has been going since 1976, has SRA authorisation, Conveyancing Quality Scheme accreditation, Children Law accreditation, three offices, five-star Google reviews and a 24-hour social services emergency line. These are real selling points that set the firm apart from a lot of competition. But on the current homepage, most of this is buried — you have to scroll a long way to find the reviews, the accreditation logos are small in the footer, and the 24-hour emergency line is only mentioned once in a small topbar. Someone who lands on the site from Google and doesn't scroll probably leaves without seeing any of it.
✓ Fixed in the rebuildThe rebuilt site leads with the firm's credentials from the first second: established 1976, SRA regulated, CQS and Children Law accreditations, three local offices, and the 24hr emergency line in a clearly visible urgent banner. Accreditations appear in the hero, trust strip, about page and footer.
