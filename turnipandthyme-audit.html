What's Going Wrong

🐌
The site takes nearly 15 seconds to load on mobile
Critical · Losing Customers

What's happening

Google measured the time until your main content appears on a standard mobile connection — a figure called Largest Contentful Paint. For your site, that's 14.9 seconds. First Contentful Paint (when anything at all shows on screen) is 7.5 seconds. Both are in the red zone.

What this means for you

Most people give up on a website within 3–4 seconds if it hasn't loaded. At 14.9 seconds, the vast majority of people browsing Turnip & Thyme on their phone will leave before they've seen a single thing. Someone looking for a bistro on Ecclesall Road will simply go to the next result instead. Given you have a seafood night, steak night, Sunday roasts and award wins to shout about, it's a shame for any of that to go unseen.

The fix

A properly built site optimises every image, loads only what's needed, and hits sub-3-second load times even with beautiful food photography.

📦
Your page weighs 4.4MB — mostly unnecessary images
Critical · Slow Load

What's happening

Google flagged a total network payload of 4,401 KiB — that's 4.4MB of data your visitors have to download before the page works. Of that, images alone could be reduced by 2,443 KiB (about 2.4MB) without any visible quality loss, just by serving them at the right size and in a modern format. On top of that, there's 359 KiB of unused JavaScript that loads on every page visit.

What this means for you

On a mobile connection, 4.4MB is a very heavy page. Most news websites are under 1MB. Every extra MB adds seconds to the load time and uses up your customers' mobile data. It's the single biggest reason for the slow load times above.

The fix

Images are compressed, resized to what's actually displayed, and served in modern formats (WebP/AVIF). JavaScript is kept to only what the page needs. A rebuilt site typically comes in well under 500KB total.

🔍
Customers can't zoom in on your site
Accessibility · Affects Real Users

What's happening

Your site has a line of code in it that explicitly disables pinch-to-zoom on mobile devices: user-scalable=0, maximum-scale=1.0. This means anyone who needs to zoom in — whether to read your menu prices, see the small print, or simply because they have a visual impairment — physically cannot do so.

What this means for you

This affects a significant proportion of users, particularly older customers who increase text size on their phones as a matter of habit. It's also flagged by Google as an accessibility failure, contributing to your 72 accessibility score. It's the kind of thing that quietly frustrates people without them knowing why.

The fix

Remove the restriction entirely. A well-built responsive site doesn't need to block zoom — it works correctly at any size.

👆
Buttons and links are too small to tap on mobile
Accessibility · Mobile UX

What's happening

Google flagged that touch targets — buttons, links, and tappable elements — don't have sufficient size or spacing on mobile. This means customers tapping "Book a table" or a menu link on their phone may hit the wrong thing, or struggle to tap it at all.

What this means for you

Every time a customer fumbles with a button on your site, there's a chance they give up. For a booking button in particular, that fumble could cost you a table. It's a small thing that makes a real difference to how professional and usable the site feels.

The fix

All interactive elements are sized to at least 44×44px with proper spacing — the standard recommended by both Google and Apple for mobile usability.

⚡
Render-blocking code is slowing the first load
Performance · 2 Second Delay

What's happening

Google flagged render-blocking requests that delay the page appearing by an estimated 2,030 milliseconds — over 2 seconds. This means scripts or stylesheets are loading before the browser is allowed to show anything to the visitor. This is a common WordPress issue caused by plugins and themes loading code in the wrong order.

What this means for you

Those 2 seconds are added on top of all the other delays — they're 2 seconds where your customers are staring at a blank white screen before anything has even started loading.

The fix

A bespoke build loads styles and scripts in the correct order, deferred where possible, so the browser can start rendering the page immediately.

🏗️
WordPress + plugins adding unnecessary overhead
Platform Limitation

What's happening

Your site is built on WordPress, which is a perfectly solid platform — but it comes with performance overhead from the core system, your theme, and any plugins installed. Google found 8 long main-thread tasks, 10 non-composited animations, and 31 KiB of unused CSS — all typical of a WordPress setup that's grown organically over time.

What this means for you

None of this is easily fixable within WordPress without significant technical work. The platform itself contributes to the weight and slowness, regardless of how good the content is.

The fix

A lightweight bespoke build has none of this overhead — just clean HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript. Nothing loading that isn't needed.
