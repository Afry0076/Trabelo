What's not working

Seven findings, worst first

1
Critical · fix today

Template placeholder text on your most sensitive service

What's happening

Under "Unattended Deaths & Clean Ups Following A Death" on your bio-hazard page, the unedited Squarespace placeholder is still in place. Two further headings — Crime Scene Cleaning and Mental Health Crisis — have no text under them at all.

What this means for your customers

These are the pages people reach at the worst moment of their lives, usually at night, usually in a hurry. What they find is an unfinished page. The natural conclusion is that the business is unfinished too — so they ring a national franchise instead, and that job was worth several hundred pounds to you.

The fix

Write the three missing descriptions properly and sensitively. In the demo rebuild these are already drafted for you to approve or change.

2
Critical

Your highest-value service is invisible on your homepage

What's happening

You've told us bio-hazard cleaning is the focus of your business. Your Facebook cover says the same — bio-hazard, mental health cleans, trauma and decomposition. Your homepage doesn't contain the words "bio-hazard", "trauma" or "specialist" anywhere. The page is instead topped by a scrolling banner advertising cleaning products, repeated four times. To find the bio-hazard page you must open a hamburger menu and pick the third item, on a URL that ends /biohazard-cleaning-services-1.

What this means for your customers

Anyone who lands on your homepage looking for trauma or hoarding cleaning assumes you're a domestic cleaner and leaves. Meanwhile the trailing "-1" on that URL is the sign of a duplicate page, which means Google may well be ranking the wrong one — or splitting you across both.

The fix

Split the business into its two honest halves on the homepage — everyday cleaning and specialist bio-hazard — with the specialist side given equal weight and its own clean URL, plus a phone-first emergency prompt. That's exactly what the demo does.

3
Critical

13.2 seconds before your content appears on a phone

What's happening

Google measures your Largest Contentful Paint — the moment the main thing on screen finishes loading — at 13.2 seconds on a mid-range Android over 4G. First paint is 4.8 seconds. Behind it: 740ms of render-blocking requests, 799 KiB of unoptimised images, 354 KiB of unused JavaScript and 135 KiB of unused CSS.

What this means for your customers

Most people abandon a page after about three seconds. On a phone — which is where your customers are, standing in a kitchen they want cleaned — the majority never see your site at all. They see a blank white screen, then they leave. You never find out it happened.

The fix

A hand-coded static site with no page-builder overhead. For comparison, Trabelo's own site loads its main content in 1.5 seconds on the same test, and the demo we've built for you is built the same way.

4
High

Three email addresses and two phone numbers

What's happening

Depending on where somebody finds you, they're given a different way to contact you:

Website footer → Enquiriessqueakyclean@outlook.com
Facebook contact info → squeakycleanvb@outlook.com
Facebook cover image → squeakycleantheonlywaytogleam@gmail.com

Your homepage also carries two different mobile numbers — 07507 354681 and 07936 507936.

What this means for your customers

Enquiries are landing in inboxes you may not be watching. If one of those three is checked less often, some of your leads are simply sitting unread — and you'd have no way of knowing. Two phone numbers also makes a business look like it's changed hands, which matters when you're asking someone to trust you with a key to their house.

The fix

Pick one address and one number, put them everywhere, and forward the other two. Ten minutes of work, and it may be the highest-return job on this list.

5
High

What Google actually shows when someone searches for you

What's happening

Your homepage title tag is 262 characters long — Google shows about 60. It's a marketing sentence that gets cut off mid-thought:

Squeaky Clean Delivers excellent cleaning services in a concentrated geographical area to Penistone and the local area in South & West Yorkshire, our goal is to provide each of our clients with a safe

The description underneath it — the grey text in Google results — is a list of place names with broken punctuation that ends mid-word:

Delivering excellent cleaning services in Millhouse green Oxspring , Holmfirth, Oughtibridge,Inbirchworth ,Denbydale,Green Moor, Stainborough , Springvale, Highgreen, Ecclesfield ,Chapeltown, Sheffield , Darton, Baraugh Gree

Your inner pages are worse — each one has the raw web address pasted into its title: "Contact Us https://www.squeaky-clean.co.uk Squeaky Clean".

What this means for your customers

That mess is your shop window in Google. It's the only thing a stranger sees before deciding whether to click you or the company below you. "Baraugh Gree" is also a misspelling of Barugh Green — as is "Stocksksbridge" for Stocksbridge and "Inbirchworth" for Ingbirchworth, both of which appear on your live pages. Misspelling the towns you serve is the one thing local search really can't forgive.

The fix

A proper 55-character title and a written description for every page, with the area names spelled correctly and used as genuine local SEO rather than a keyword dump.

6
High

Your proof is on Facebook. Your website has none of it.

What's happening

Your Accreditations & Qualifications page contains one line of text — "Trademark Registration Certificate" — and nothing else. No logos, no certificates, no DBS or insurance detail. Yet your Facebook cover carries the Federation of Master Cleaners, the Domestic Cleaning Alliance and DCBN badges, and your Facebook page holds 35 reviews with a 100% recommendation rate. Not one of those reviews appears on your website.

What this means for your customers

You are asking people to let you into their home, or to handle a death in the family, and the page that's meant to prove you're qualified is empty. Meanwhile the strongest evidence you own — a decade of genuine recommendations and three industry memberships — is sitting on a platform plenty of your older commercial customers never open.

The fix

Reviews on the homepage, accreditation badges beside them, and a real accreditations page with the certificates on it. All of that is already laid out in the demo, waiting for your logo files.

7
Medium

Your contact page has no way to contact you

What's happening

The Contact Us page is a bare form with six required fields. No phone number, no email address, no address, no map, no hours. Your Facebook says you're always open — the website never mentions it. Elsewhere, 16 of your 20 images have no alt text, several links have no readable name, and some text fails Google's colour contrast check, which is what's holding Accessibility at 89.

What this means for your customers

Someone with an emergency — a flood, sharps found in a garden, a death — does not want to fill in a six-field form. They want a number to ring, and if they can't see one in about five seconds they ring somebody else. That's your highest-value work walking away at the last step.

The fix

Phone number in the header of every page, tappable on a phone, plus a contact page that leads with the number and keeps the form for the jobs that aren't urgent.
