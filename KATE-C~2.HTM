What's Not Working — and Why It Matters

Four specific issues found on your site, in plain terms.

Critical — Performance

Performance score of 27 — main content takes 15 seconds to load on mobile

What's happening

Google's mobile test shows the Largest Contentful Paint — the main content — takes 15.2 seconds to appear. Your first contentful paint (the first thing that shows up) takes 6.2 seconds. There's 2,430 milliseconds of total blocking time — meaning the browser is completely frozen for over 2 seconds before anything can happen. The site is loading 929KB of unused JavaScript and a total of 3.2MB of data on every visit. There are 20 long main-thread tasks. This is a Squarespace limitation — the platform loads everything regardless of whether the current page needs it.

What this means for your clients

A new client searching "gym Sheffield S11" or "pregnancy fitness Sheffield" on their phone is waiting 15 seconds before they can read about your free taster session or Kate's background. More than half of mobile users leave in under 3 seconds. The specialisms, the warmth, the breadth of services — none of it gets a chance to land.

The fix

A hand-built site with no Squarespace overhead, no unused code, optimised images, and no render-blocking scripts. Target performance score: 97+. Load time: under 2 seconds.

Critical — Performance

Scripts blocking the page from loading — 1,300ms delay before anything appears

What's happening

Google found render-blocking requests with a potential saving of 1,300 milliseconds. These are scripts loading in the page header that force the browser to stop and wait before it can show anything. It also found 125KB of unused CSS — style code being downloaded but never applied to your page.

What this means for your clients

Before a single word or image can appear, the browser is waiting 1.3 seconds downloading files it may not need. Combined with the 15-second load time above, this means the site feels slow from the first moment.

The fix

No render-blocking scripts. All CSS and JavaScript loaded only when needed, non-blocking. A clean build from scratch.

Accessibility

Colour contrast and link labels — some clients find it harder to read

What's happening

Google found that some background and foreground colours don't have a sufficient contrast ratio — meaning text against its background is harder to read for people with visual impairments. It also found links without discernible names (likely image or icon links with no label) and heading elements not in the correct descending order.

What this means for your clients

Kate Campbell Fitness serves people at every stage of life — teens, new mums, older adults. For a studio that takes inclusion seriously, a fully accessible site is an extension of that approach. Low contrast particularly affects older adults and anyone with low vision.

The fix

Full contrast compliance, all links and images properly labelled, correct heading structure. Accessibility target: 98+.

SEO

4 links without descriptive text — affecting how Google reads your site

What's happening

Google found 4 links with no descriptive text — these are typically image buttons or icons where the label that tells Google (and screen readers) what the link does is missing.

What this means for your clients

Google uses link text to understand how pages connect and what they're about. Missing labels mean Google is working with incomplete information about your site — which can hold back your ranking for searches like "gym Ecclesall Road" or "postnatal fitness Sheffield".

The fix

All links properly labelled throughout. SEO target: 100.
