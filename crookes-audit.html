Found by hand

Five things a speed test won't tell you.

Automated tools measure how a page loads. They can't tell whether the What's On page is actually current, or whether Google can see your gigs at all. These came from going through the site page by page — re-checked on 21 August 2026.

1. Google cannot see a single one of your events.

Biggest opportunity
What's happening

Your site contains no structured data whatsoever — zero JSON-LD blocks. For a music venue that is the single most costly thing on this list. Structured data is how you tell Google "this is an Event, here's the act, the date, the price and the ticket link."

What this means for the club

When someone searches "gigs in Sheffield this weekend" or "live music near me tonight", Google shows an events panel at the top of the results. Venues with Event schema appear in it. You don't — so Explosive Light Orchestra, Kat Eaton, the Comedy Village and every jazz night are invisible to the single biggest source of new audience there is. You're relying entirely on people who already know your name.

The same applies to voice: ask Siri or Google "what's on at Crookes Club this week" and there's nothing there for it to read.

The fix

Event schema generated automatically from your TicketSource feed, plus LocalBusiness schema for the club itself. Add a gig on TicketSource and it becomes eligible for Google's event results without anyone touching the website.

2. The What's On page is out of date — and updating it is harder than it should be.

Critical
What's happening

The events on the site are a mix of listings with no dates at all, and dates that have already passed. Meanwhile you're actively selling tickets on TicketSource — the two aren't connected, so the website is permanently behind.

What this means for the club

Someone checking the site sees an out-of-date list and concludes nothing much is happening. It's the worst possible impression for a venue whose whole selling point is something on every night. And because adding an event means manually uploading images and typing links, it understandably doesn't always get done — so the problem repeats itself.

The fix

This is exactly what the TicketSource integration solves, and it's why the £795 option is the right one for you. Add the gig once, on TicketSource, where you're already adding it. The website updates itself.

3. Eight people's personal mobile numbers are published in plain text.

High
What's happening

The Regular Activities page lists the personal mobile numbers of the individuals who run your classes — toddler sensory, baby sensory, Zumba, French, self-defence, Brownies, gamers' night and more — as plain readable text.

What this means for the club

Plain-text numbers on a public page get harvested by automated scrapers within weeks; that's where a lot of spam calls originate. These aren't club numbers, they're private individuals' phones, published on your site. It's worth tidying on their behalf.

There's a commercial angle too: every one of those is an enquiry that bypasses the club entirely. You never hear about it, and you can't follow up.

The fix

Route class enquiries through the club, with organisers named but not exposed. Protects them, and you keep sight of the enquiry. This is already built in the demo — see the Activities page.

What this means for the club

These are searchable in their own right — "jazz Sheffield Friday", "comedy night Sheffield", "stand-up S10". Each deserves a real page that can rank on its own and bring in people who've never heard of Crookes Club but are looking for exactly what you already do every week. An empty page ranks for nothing.

The fix

Proper pages for both, included in the £795 option. Both are built in the demo.

5. Half your images are invisible to Google, and the site still says © 2018.

Opportunity
What's happening

11 of your 22 images have no alt text, so Google can't tell what any of them show, and anyone using a screen reader gets nothing. The footer still reads © 2018. Google also flags buttons without labels and text that's hard to read — that's what pulls Accessibility down to 76 on desktop.

What this means for the club

Photos of a packed concert room are strong evidence, and right now they carry no search value at all. The © 2018 line is a small thing that quietly tells every visitor the site was abandoned years ago.

The fix

All images described, all controls labelled, contrast fixed, and a year that updates itself. Accessibility 98+ across both mobile and desktop.
