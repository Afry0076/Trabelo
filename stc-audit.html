Hi — I'm Aidan. I'm 18 and I do web design across Sheffield and South Yorkshire. I came across Sheffield Therapy Centre while looking at healthcare and physio websites in the area, ran your site through Google's speed testing tool and found a few things worth flagging.

You've got a solid SEO score of 92 — so you're showing up in Google searches, which matters. But when people actually land on the site, they're running into some friction that's worth fixing. The biggest one is that your site is loading nearly 12 seconds of content on mobile — in a sector where people are usually in discomfort and just want to book quickly. Here's a plain-English breakdown of what I found.

What's Not Working — and Why It Matters

Five specific issues found on your current site, explained in terms of what each one means for the people trying to use it.

Critical

The main content takes nearly 12 seconds to load on mobile

What's happening

Google found that the Largest Contentful Paint — that's the main block of content on the page — takes 11.7 seconds to appear on a mobile device. The first thing to appear takes 2.9 seconds. Render-blocking files are adding nearly 3 extra seconds of delay before the page even starts loading. The site is also loading nearly 5MB of data on every single visit — which is very large for a healthcare website.

What this means for your patients

Someone's been putting off their physio appointment, they've just searched "physiotherapy Sheffield" on their phone, found your site and... waited 12 seconds. Studies consistently show that over 50% of mobile users leave if a site takes more than 3 seconds to load. Your potential patients are making appointment decisions in that window. Every second matters — and 12 is a lot to ask.

The fix

A rebuilt site eliminates render-blocking files, compresses images properly (Google found 709KB of image savings available), and loads in under 2 seconds on mobile. Target performance score: 97+.

Critical

Best Practices score of 54 — partly because the site makes insecure (HTTP) requests

What's happening

Google flagged 3 insecure HTTP requests on your site — meaning some files are being loaded without encryption. In plain terms, parts of your site aren't using HTTPS (the secure version of the web). On top of that, Google found browser errors, deprecated APIs being used, and several missing security headers (CSP, HSTS, COOP and X-Frame-Options). Together, these drag your Best Practices score down to 54 out of 100.

What this means for your patients

Modern browsers increasingly warn users when a site isn't fully secure. For a healthcare clinic where patients might be entering personal details or contact information, a security warning — or even just a lock icon that isn't green — will put some people off. Trust is the foundation of any clinic; your website should reflect that.

The fix

A rebuilt site is built fully on HTTPS with all security headers configured correctly from day one. Target score: 90+.

Critical

725KB of unused JavaScript is slowing the site down on every visit

What's happening

Google found 725KB of unused JavaScript and 174KB of unused CSS being loaded by your site — code that's downloaded on every single visit but never actually used. There are also 5 long main-thread tasks blocking the browser. This is a very common symptom of a WordPress or page-builder site that's accumulated plugins over time.

What this means for your patients

All that dead code adds delay. The browser has to download, parse and evaluate code it doesn't need — before it can show the patient anything. It's invisible to you, but every patient visiting your site is paying for it in waiting time.

The fix

A hand-built site with no bloated frameworks, no unnecessary plugins and only the code that's actually needed. Clean, minimal and fast.

Accessibility

Contrast issues and missing labels mean some visitors can't fully use the site

What's happening

Google found that some text on your site doesn't have a high enough contrast ratio against its background — making it harder to read, especially for people with visual impairments. There are also iframes (embedded content) without titles, and headings that skip levels, which affects screen-reader navigation. These issues drop your Accessibility score to 89.

What this means for your patients

Physiotherapy and podiatry clinics treat a lot of patients who are older or who have conditions that affect their vision or mobility. These are exactly the people who rely on accessible websites. Good accessibility isn't just a technical checkbox — it's part of looking after your patients before they even walk through the door.

The fix

A properly structured site with sufficient colour contrast, all content correctly labelled and a logical heading structure. Target accessibility score: 98+.

SEO

Three links have no descriptive text — slightly hurting your SEO and usability

What's happening

Google found three links on your site with no descriptive text — likely image links or buttons where the text is missing. These are flagged as an SEO issue because Google can't understand what those links point to. Same goes for screen readers, which read out link text to visually impaired visitors.

What this means for your patients

A small issue on its own — but combined with the other accessibility points, it adds up to a site that's harder to use for some visitors. It also means Google is slightly less confident in understanding your site's structure, which can affect rankings.

The fix

All links properly labelled with descriptive text. Standard on any cleanly built site.
