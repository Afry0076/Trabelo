What's Not Working — and Why It Matters

Three specific issues found on your site, in plain terms.

Performance

Performance score of 75 — your page takes 5 seconds to load on a phone

What's happening

Google's mobile test shows your Largest Contentful Paint — the main content — takes 5 seconds to appear. There's also 503KB of unused JavaScript being downloaded on every visit, 2.87MB of total data, and 6 long blocking tasks that freeze the browser before anyone can interact with the page. This is typical of Wix-built sites — the platform loads a lot of code it doesn't actually use.

What this means for your members

Someone searching "women's gym Sheffield" on their phone is waiting 5 seconds before they can read a word. The 30-day trial, the small group format, the money-back guarantee — none of it makes an impression until the page loads. Over half of mobile users leave within 3 seconds. The offer is strong. The site just needs to load fast enough to show it.

The fix

A hand-built site with no Wix bloat, only the code that's needed, properly compressed images, and no render-blocking scripts. Target performance score: 97+. Load time: under 2 seconds.

SEO

Missing meta description — Google is guessing what to show about your gym

What's happening

Your site has no meta description. This is the short sentence Google shows under your site name in search results. Without one, Google pulls random text from your page — which often looks unprofessional and doesn't give people a reason to click.

What this means for your members

Someone searching "women's gym Sheffield S8" sees your site in the results but the description is whatever Google grabbed — not your best selling line. A good meta description is free advertising in search results. Without one, you're leaving that space blank.

The fix

A clean meta description on every page and proper SEO structure throughout. Target SEO score: 100.

Accessibility

Minor accessibility issues — heading structure and ARIA labels

What's happening

Google flagged that heading elements aren't in a proper descending order, and some ARIA attributes — the labels used by screen readers and assistive technology — have prohibited values. These are minor but they contribute to the 95 accessibility score rather than a perfect 100.

What this means for your members

Most members won't notice, but for anyone using a screen reader to browse your site, the experience is slightly harder to navigate. For a gym that specifically trains members through pregnancy and menopause — a broad range of needs — a fully accessible site shows you mean it when you say everyone is welcome.

The fix

Correct heading hierarchy, all ARIA attributes properly set, full semantic structure. Accessibility target: 98+.
