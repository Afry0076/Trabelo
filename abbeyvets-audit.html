What's Going Wrong

Here's what Google flagged — in plain English, with what it means for Abbey Vet Group and your clients.

🐌
Critical · Performance
13.6 seconds before your main content loads — in Google's test

What's happening

Google's lab test measured a Largest Contentful Paint (the time until the main visible content appears on screen) of 13.6 seconds on mobile. The main cause is 2,270ms of render-blocking resources — code that forces the browser to stop loading everything else while it processes CSS and JavaScript. A second issue is image delivery: 1,156 KiB of images are being sent in formats or sizes that aren't optimised for mobile.

Good news — real-world data is better

Your field LCP (from real user visits) is 1.4 seconds, which passes Google's Core Web Vitals threshold. This means your regular visitors load the site faster than the lab test suggests — likely because browsers cache certain resources after the first visit. The problem is that first-visit experience, and the lab score, which influences how Google weighs your site in search rankings.

The fix

A rebuilt site eliminates render-blocking resources entirely, serves images in WebP format at the right dimensions, and loads in under 2 seconds even on a first visit. Target: 97+ performance, under 2s LCP on mobile.

🖼️
Performance · Images
1,156 KB of images could be delivered much more efficiently

What's happening

Google found 1,156 KB of images that could be compressed and served in modern formats (WebP or AVIF) with no visible quality loss. On top of that, the browser is receiving images that are larger than the screen dimensions they're displayed at — unnecessary data being transferred on every page load.

What this means for your clients

Every extra megabyte adds load time — especially on mobile data. Clients viewing your site on a 4G connection in Darton or Royston are downloading images that are larger than they need to be. For a multi-branch directory page that lists all 8 locations, this kind of overhead compounds quickly.

The fix

A rebuilt site serves every image in WebP format, sized to the exact display dimensions. Typically this reduces image payload by 60–80% with no visible difference to the user.

♿
Accessibility
Images without alt text, heading hierarchy and contrast items

What's happening

Three specific accessibility issues were flagged: some images don't have alt text (meaning screen readers can't describe them to visually impaired users), heading tags don't follow a logical hierarchy (which affects navigation for assistive technology users), and some text/background colour combinations don't meet the contrast minimum.

What this means for your clients

For clients who use screen readers — including visually impaired pet owners who may visit any of your 8 branches — these issues mean certain parts of the site are harder or impossible to navigate. Given the size and reach of Abbey Vet Group, this affects more users than a single-branch practice would.

The fix

A rebuild with accessibility as a design principle — every image gets alt text, heading hierarchy is correct from the start, and contrast is checked against WCAG AA throughout. Target: Accessibility 98+.

🎬
Accessibility · Video
Video content without captions

What's happening

There is video on your site that doesn't have captions or a text transcript. This is flagged by Google as an accessibility issue, and is a WCAG requirement for pre-recorded video content.

The fix

Video content on a rebuilt site includes captions or a linked transcript. Where video isn't required, a high-quality static image serves the same purpose without the accessibility overhead.

🔗
SEO · Accessibility
Links without descriptive text — affecting SEO and screen reader users

What's happening

Some links on the site don't have meaningful descriptive text — for example, links labelled "click here" or icons with no text alternative. Google uses link text as a signal for what the linked page is about, and screen readers read this text aloud to users.

The fix

Every link on a rebuilt site has descriptive text — "Book an appointment at our Darton branch" rather than "click here." This benefits both SEO and any client using assistive technology.
