Open your homepage on a phone and press "Make an Appointment Now". It takes you to lessthanzerobarbers.com/booksy, and a first-time visitor sees this:

Log In · New to this site? Sign Up · Email · Password · Forgot password?

That's a Wix members' log-in page. The same button on your Barbershops page — and the "Powered by Booksy & Fresha" link beside it — goes to the same place. So does "Pre Authorisation" in your main menu. Someone who wants a skin fade on Saturday is asked to create an account on your website before they can get anywhere near Booksy.

Your regulars won't notice, because they book straight through the Booksy or Fresha app. New customers are exactly the people who come through the website — and they're the ones hitting the wall.

What's not working

Seven findings, worst first.

1
Critical · check today

Your booking button leads to a log-in page

What's happening

"Make an Appointment Now" on the homepage and Barbershops page, "Powered by Booksy & Fresha", and "Pre Authorisation" in the main menu all land on a Wix sign-in wall. On the Woodseats page, the only link labelled Booksy — the "Booksy Reviews" heading — goes to Instagram instead.

What this costs you

The website's single most important job is turning a stranger into a booking, and right now that path is blocked at the last step. Every new customer who gives up at the log-in screen books with the barber down the road instead — and never shows up in any number you can see.

The fix

Point every booking button straight at each shop's own Booksy or Fresha page — no account, no detour. The demo has a "pick your shop" booking page that does exactly this, one tap per shop.

2
Critical

8.9 seconds to load on a phone

What's happening

Your homepage's main content takes 8.9 seconds to appear on a mid-range phone over 4G, and the page is frozen for 870ms while scripts run. The page weighs 3,437 KiB, of which 701 KiB is JavaScript that's never used. On top of Wix's own code, the page loads Google Analytics, Tag Manager, a Facebook pixel, Sentry error tracking and two separate third-party widget scripts.

What this costs you

Most people give a page about three seconds. The ones searching "barbers near me" on their phone are comparing you against every other result on the screen — and the one that loads first gets the tap.

The fix

A hand-coded site with no page-builder overhead and only the tracking you actually use. For comparison, Trabelo's own site loads its main content in 1.5 seconds on the same test — and the demo is built the same way.

3
High

The email address you show isn't where it goes

What's happening

On your homepage and contact page, a link reads info@lessthanzerobarbers.com — but clicking it opens an email to a different address entirely:

Shows:  info@lessthanzerobarbers.com
Sends to: ltzbarbers@aol.co.uk

What this costs you

If that AOL inbox isn't checked as closely as the info@ address, training enquiries, apprenticeship applications and business emails are going somewhere nobody's looking. Nobody will ever tell you — they'll just assume you didn't reply.

The fix

One-line change: make the link match the address. Worth checking the AOL inbox today in case anything's already waiting there.

4
High

A customer lands on a training company's homepage

What's happening

The main headline on your homepage is "Developing the next generation of barbers, professionals & business leaders". The page title Google shows is:

LTZ Group - Training - Barbers - FAF, Sheffield & Chesterfield

"FAF" means nothing to someone looking for a haircut, and Woodseats isn't mentioned. Behind the scenes the homepage has five separate H1 headings and no H2s, so Google has no clear idea what the main subject of the page is.

What this costs you

The group genuinely is both — a barbering business and an education business. But the two audiences want different things, and the homepage currently talks to the smaller one first. A customer wanting a cut, a price and a time has to work to find any of the three.

The fix

Lead with the chair — cuts, shops, booking — and give the academy its own clearly signposted front door. The demo does both, and adds a two-question "route finder" so trainees land on the right programme without reading every page.

5
High

No opening hours or prices anywhere

What's happening

We checked the homepage, Barbershops, Woodseats, About and Contact pages. None of them show opening hours for any of your three shops, and none show a single price. The Woodseats page is almost entirely a reviews feed.

What this costs you

"Are they open now?" and "how much is a skin fade?" are the two questions every new customer has. If the website can't answer them, they ring — or, more often, they go to Google Maps and pick whoever's listing shows the hours.

The fix

Hours and a price list on every shop page, with a live "open now" indicator. The demo leaves slots for both, because we won't publish figures we don't have.

6
Medium

Typos on the page that sells your services

What's happening

Your Barbershops page — the one describing the haircuts — currently reads:

"…tailored to your style, haiur tyoe and lifesycle, no matter what LTZ Group brnd you choose"
"Whether youre after a modern taper fade, skinfade ,burst fade…"
"More than 2800 verified reviews across Google, Boosky & Fresha"

The contact page also lists your Woodseats manager as "Luke DIlly".

What this costs you

You sell precision and attention to detail — "Boosky" in the sentence about your 2,800 reviews undercuts exactly the thing that sentence is trying to prove.

The fix

Ten minutes of proofreading. The demo's copy is rewritten from yours, cleanly.

7
Medium

The smaller things Google notices

What's happening

Lighthouse flags images without alt text and links with no readable name — by our count, around ten homepage links have no text a screen reader or search engine can read. The browser logs errors to the console. And two different Instagram accounts are linked across the site: @lessthanzerobarbers in the footer and @ltzbarbers on the Woodseats page.

What this costs you

Individually minor. Together they tell Google the site isn't well maintained — and they're the difference between a 92 SEO score and a 100.

The fix

Handled as standard in a rebuild: every image described, every link named, one set of social accounts.
