What's Not Working — and Why It Matters

Four specific issues found on your site, in plain terms.

Critical

Performance score of 42 — main content takes 15 seconds to load on mobile

What's happening

Google's mobile test shows the Largest Contentful Paint is 15.2 seconds — meaning the main content of your page takes over 15 seconds to appear on a phone. The first thing to appear takes 10.2 seconds. There are 17 long main-thread tasks, 500KB of unused JavaScript, 75KB of unused CSS, and 412KB of savings available on images alone. The site is loading over 3MB of data per visit.

What this means for your patients

You've won Physiotherapy Clinic of the Year three years running. But a patient searching "physiotherapy Sheffield" on their phone and landing on your site at 10pm is waiting 15 seconds before they can read a single word. Over half of mobile users leave after 3 seconds. Your awards, reviews and specialist equipment never get a chance to make an impression — the site gives up before they do.

The fix

A clean rebuild with no bloated WordPress plugins, properly compressed images, no unused CSS or JS, and no render-blocking files. Target performance score: 97+. Load time: under 2 seconds.

Critical

500KB of unused JavaScript and 3.1 seconds of JavaScript execution time on every visit

What's happening

Google found 500KB of unused JavaScript being loaded on every page visit — code the browser downloads, parses and evaluates before it can show anything. JavaScript execution takes 3.1 seconds and main-thread work takes 4.4 seconds. This is a classic symptom of a WordPress site with accumulated plugins over time.

What this means for your patients

Every millisecond of JavaScript execution is time your patient spends staring at a blank or partial screen. 17 long blocking tasks means the browser is repeatedly freezing — the site feels sluggish even once it does load.

The fix

A hand-built site with only the code that's needed — no page builder, no plugin bloat. Fast by design.

Accessibility

Several accessibility issues — viewport zooming disabled and missing labels

What's happening

Google found that the site uses user-scalable="no" in the viewport tag — meaning users cannot pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This is a specific accessibility violation that affects people with visual impairments who rely on zooming. There are also iframes without titles, links without discernible names, and select elements without labels. These issues bring accessibility to 86 out of 100.

What this means for your patients

Disabling zoom is particularly problematic for a healthcare website. Patients trying to read your treatment details or prices on a phone and can't make the text bigger. For a clinic that treats patients of all ages, this is the opposite of the welcoming, accessible experience you want to provide.

The fix

No zoom restrictions, all elements properly labelled, full semantic structure. Target: 98+.

SEO

7 links with no descriptive text — affecting how Google reads your site

What's happening

Google flagged 7 links on your site with no descriptive text — these are likely image links or icon buttons where the accessible label is missing. Google uses link text to understand what pages link to, and screen readers use it to describe links to visually impaired users.

What this means for your patients

On its own this is a minor issue — but combined with the accessibility flags above, your SEO score of 92 has room to reach 100, and these gaps are part of what's holding it back.

The fix

All links properly labelled throughout. SEO target: 100.
