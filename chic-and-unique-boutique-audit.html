Hi — I'm Aidan, I do web design across Sheffield and South Yorkshire. I'll be straight with you: your site isn't broken. It's on Shopify, it's clean, it's on-brand, and your SEO (92) and search-crawlability are both genuinely solid — you're not invisible on Google. That's rarer than you'd think for a boutique your size, so credit where it's due.

But "solid" and "working against you" aren't opposites. Google's own test on your site shows a real gap between desktop (Performance 94) and mobile (Performance 71, main content taking 8.5 seconds to appear) — and most of your customers are finding you on their phone, not a desktop. That gap, plus a handful of accessibility issues, is what a fully custom-built storefront is designed to close. Here's exactly what's happening, and what I'd build instead.

What's Holding the Current Site Back

A generic Shopify theme is built to work for thousands of different shops — which means it's never built specifically for yours. This is where that shows up, in Google's own numbers.

Performance

8.5 seconds before a mobile shopper sees a single product

What's happening

Google's mobile test measures a Largest Contentful Paint of 8.5 seconds, with 941 KiB of potential savings flagged directly on image delivery, and a total mobile page weight of 3.3MB. On desktop the same page loads in 1.2 seconds — the theme simply isn't built mobile-first.

What this means for your customers

Someone taps through from a Google search or an Instagram link on their phone, and waits over 8 seconds before they see anything to buy. A meaningful share of that traffic won't wait — they're gone before your products even render. And because Google ranks mobile pages on speed, this score itself is part of why you're harder to find.

The fix

A storefront built mobile-first from the ground up, with images sized and loaded properly for every device — targeting Performance 97+ instead of 71.

Accessibility

Some customers are being locked out without anyone noticing

What's happening

Google flags missing alt text on product images, text/background colour combinations below contrast minimums, touch targets too small for a thumb on mobile, and a heading order that skips levels across the site.

What this means for your customers

Screen-reader users and low-vision shoppers have a genuinely harder time browsing and buying — that's real lost sales, not a technicality. It also quietly affects Google Image Search, since alt text is one of the signals it uses to understand and surface product photos.

The fix

Built to proper accessibility standards from day one — Accessibility 98+ instead of 84, with every image, colour and heading done right the first time.

Under the Hood

The page loads more code than it ever uses

What's happening

Google flags 39 KiB of unused CSS and 32 KiB of unused JavaScript, plus render-blocking requests costing around 150ms before the page can even start drawing — most likely leftover theme features and apps you're not actively using.

What this means for your customers

It compounds everything above — every extra kilobyte is more time spent downloading before anything useful appears, especially off wifi.

The fix

A lean, purpose-built front end with nothing loaded that isn't actually used.

Already Good — Kept

Your SEO, Shopify backend and AI-shopping readiness stay exactly as they are

What's happening

SEO scores 92 on both mobile and desktop, and the site scores 4/4 on mobile Agentic Browsing — Google's newer check for how well AI shopping assistants can read and use a site. Your cart, checkout, payments and stock all run through Shopify, and work fine.

What this means for you

None of this needs replacing. A rebuild here means a new front door on a foundation that already works — not starting your shop over from nothing.
