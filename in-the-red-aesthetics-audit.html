Hi Millie — I'm Aidan. I do web design across Sheffield and South Yorkshire, and I came across In The Red Aesthetics on Facebook. That logo alone is stronger branding than most clinics twice your size have — genuinely a shame it's not being used anywhere except social media.

The link you share to book — facesconsent.com — isn't really your own website. It's a shared booking platform's clinic page, and I ran it through Google's testing tool. The results explain a lot: a performance score of 32 out of 100, and — more seriously — Google is currently blocked from indexing the page at all. Here's the full picture.

What's Not Working — and Why It Matters

This is Faces Consent's platform, not something you built — so none of this reflects on you or your work. Here's what's actually happening.

Critical

Google can't index this page at all — you're invisible in search results

What's happening

Google's own test flags two things directly: "Page is blocked from indexing" and "Links are not crawlable." In plain terms, Google is not allowed to add this page to search results, and even if it could, it can't follow the links on it properly.

What this means for your clients

Someone searching "botox Rotherham," "aesthetics Dalton" or "lip filler near me" will never find you through Google — no matter how good your work is or how many 5-star reviews you get. Right now, the only way anyone finds you is if they already know to look on Facebook or Instagram, or someone tells them directly.

The fix

A real website that Google can actually index and rank — properly set up from day one, targeting the exact searches your future clients are already typing.

Critical

Performance score of 32 — the main content takes 14 seconds to appear on mobile

What's happening

Google's mobile test shows a Largest Contentful Paint of 14.0 seconds, with the page frozen or unresponsive for a combined 1,250ms across 20 separate long tasks. The page also loads 4.7MB of data — most of it, 1.5MB, is JavaScript that never actually gets used.

What this means for your clients

Someone finds your page, taps through on their phone, and waits 14 seconds before they can even see your treatment list — let alone book. For a decision as personal as aesthetic treatments, that's a long time for doubt to creep in before they've even read a word about you.

The fix

A fast, purpose-built site — target load time under 2 seconds, with your existing Faces Consent booking system linked in, not replaced.

Trust

It's a shared booking page, not your own brand

What's happening

The page is a generic clinic listing on someone else's platform — your logo, your story, and the reviews you've actually earned on Facebook don't appear anywhere on it. It shows "0 reviews" on the page itself, even though you have genuine 5-star reviews just one click away that no one visiting this page ever sees.

What this means for your clients

Aesthetic treatments are personal — people want to feel confident in who's doing them before they book. A generic booking widget with your logo missing and "0 reviews" showing does the opposite of building that confidence, even though the reality (your actual reviews, your actual results) says otherwise.

The fix

Your brand, your story and your real reviews front and centre — the confidence-building your work has already earned, actually visible.

Trust & Security

The page asks for your location before you've even chosen to do anything

What's happening

Google flags that the page requests location access automatically on load — before a visitor has clicked anything. It's also missing standard security protections (the kind that stop a page being embedded fraudulently on another site) and logs errors to the browser console.

What this means for your clients

An unexpected "allow this site to know your location?" pop-up before someone's even looked at your treatments is the kind of thing that makes people close the tab, especially anyone cautious about their privacy — not the first impression a trust-based service wants to make.

The fix

No unexpected permission requests, proper security headers set up from the start — the kind of technical hygiene that (rightly) never gets noticed because nothing goes wrong.
