What's Going Wrong

Here's what Google flagged — in plain English, with what it means for Croft Vets and your clients.

🐌
Critical · Performance
13.6 seconds to load on desktop — and 4.8 seconds on mobile

What's happening

Google's desktop lab test measured a Largest Contentful Paint (the time until the main visible content appears) of 13.6 seconds. On mobile it's 4.8 seconds. Both are well above what Google considers acceptable (2.5 seconds). The main culprits are 1.93 seconds of render-blocking resources — code that forces the browser to stop and wait before it can show anything — and 1,192 KB of JavaScript that loads but never actually gets used.

What this means for your clients

Someone searching for a vet in Totley or Bolsover who clicks your site and waits nearly 5 seconds on mobile — or nearly 14 seconds on desktop — has almost certainly clicked back and gone to a competitor before anything loaded. That's particularly costly for the kind of high-intent searches ("vets near me," "vet Totley") where someone is ready to book.

The fix

A bespoke rebuild eliminates render-blocking resources, loads only the JavaScript the site actually needs, and properly compresses and serves images. Target: 97+ performance on both mobile and desktop.

📦
Performance · Page Weight
Nearly 5 megabytes of data loading on mobile — much of it wasted

What's happening

Your total mobile page size is 4,982 KB. Google identified 1,192 KB of unused JavaScript (code that downloads but never runs), 66 KB of unused CSS, and 261 KB of images that could be compressed and delivered more efficiently. On top of that, 9 long main-thread tasks are blocking the browser from responding to the user.

What this means for your clients

Every unnecessary megabyte is wasted download time — especially on mobile data or patchy connections. It also means the browser is busy doing work it doesn't need to do, which is why clicking buttons or scrolling can feel sluggish on slower devices.

The fix

A clean bespoke build typically loads in under 1 MB. No WordPress plugin overhead, no unused libraries, images served in modern formats at the right sizes.

♿
Accessibility · Mobile
Pinching to zoom is blocked on mobile

What's happening

Your site's viewport settings include user-scalable=0 and maximum-scale=1.0 — which disables the ability for users to pinch-to-zoom on their phone. This is flagged directly by Google as an accessibility failure.

What this means for your clients

For clients with visual impairment who rely on zooming in to read text on their phones, your site is effectively unusable. That's a significant chunk of pet owners — particularly older clients who may need to increase text size to read your opening hours or phone number.

The fix

A rebuilt site never disables zooming. Mobile users can pinch, zoom and navigate exactly as they expect to.

🍪
Best Practices · Privacy
Third-party cookies flagged — a trust and compliance issue

What's happening

Google found 4 third-party cookies being set on your site. Third-party cookies are used by advertising and tracking services — they're the technology being phased out by browsers and regulators due to privacy concerns. Having them flagged in Google's audit affects your best practices score of 77 and may affect how modern browsers handle your site going forward.

What this means for you

This likely comes from embedded third-party tools (analytics, social embeds or similar). While it won't immediately cause problems for users, it's increasingly the kind of thing that erodes trust signals — and Google is factoring it into quality scores.

The fix

A rebuilt site uses only what's needed — analytics where necessary (with proper consent), nothing extraneous. No third-party tracking cookies baked in.

🏗️
Platform
Built on WordPress — performance ceilings that are hard to escape

What's happening

Your site runs on WordPress. WordPress is a general-purpose CMS used by hundreds of millions of websites — which means it loads a lot of code regardless of what your specific site actually needs. The 1,192 KB of unused JavaScript is largely a consequence of this: WordPress and its plugins load a large toolkit whether or not the current page uses it.

The fix

A lightweight bespoke build has no platform overhead. It loads exactly what's needed for your site — nothing more. This alone is typically worth 20–30 points on the performance score.
