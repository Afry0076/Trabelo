What's going wrong

Six things worth knowing about — explained plainly.

A performance score of 43 is the lowest I'd expect to see on a professionally built site. Most of it comes down to the same things: too much code loading that isn't needed, and images that haven't been optimised. All of it is fixable.

🐌
17 seconds to fully load on a phone — the worst score I've seen on a Webflow site
Performance 43 · LCP 17.3s · Speed Index 7.8s · page size 3.97MB
Think about the kind of person searching for a Sheffield wills solicitor. They might be recently bereaved and trying to get probate sorted. They might be in their 60s or 70s and finally getting round to making a will or setting up a Lasting Power of Attorney. They're on their phone, probably in the evening, and they need the site to load and be clear. At 17 seconds, most people have already pressed back before anything useful has appeared on screen. A performance score of 43 is in Google's red zone — and it means Google is actively penalising the site in search rankings, not just that it loads slowly.
✓ Fixed in the rebuildUnder 1 second load time on mobile. Performance 97+. No Webflow overhead, no unnecessary scripts — just the content the site actually needs.
🖼️
Images are adding over a second of unnecessary delay — and shifting around as the page loads
Image delivery savings of 1,012KB · layout shift from images without fixed dimensions · CLS 0.043
Google flagged two separate image problems. First, images on the site could be reduced in file size by over a megabyte without any visible quality loss — they're simply not optimised for web delivery. This alone is adding significant loading time. Second, images don't have explicit width and height set in the code, which means the browser doesn't know how big they'll be before they load — so the page jumps around as images pop in (this is called Cumulative Layout Shift, and it scores 0.043, which is just inside the "needs improvement" range). Both things make the experience worse on a phone.
✓ Fixed in the rebuildAll images properly optimised and sized. Zero layout shift. The page is stable from the moment it starts loading.
🔍
The site has no meta description — so Google writes its own, and it might not be great
SEO 92 · no meta description found · affects how the site appears in Google search results
A meta description is the short paragraph of text that appears under your firm's name when it comes up in a Google search. Without one, Google generates it automatically from whatever text it finds on the page — which is often an awkward fragment that doesn't sell the firm properly. For a firm like Rosalind Watchorn, that description should be mentioning the 30-year history, the free consultation, the CQS accreditation, and the personal service. At the moment, Google is making that call for you — and it may be getting it wrong. This is also why the SEO score is 92 rather than 100.
✓ Fixed in the rebuildA properly written meta description for every page — tailored to what someone searching for a Sheffield wills or probate solicitor is actually looking for. SEO 100.
⏱️
560ms of blocking time — scripts are making the page unresponsive before it's even loaded
Total Blocking Time 560ms · render-blocking requests saving 1,500ms · 7 long main-thread tasks
Total Blocking Time measures how long the page is locked up and unresponsive while scripts are running. 560 milliseconds is significant — it means there are over half a second of moments where a visitor has tapped something on the page and nothing has happened, because the browser is busy running scripts. Google also found 7 "long tasks" — chunks of JavaScript that take so long to run that they block everything else. This comes from third-party scripts (Webflow loads several by default) and render-blocking requests that delay the page from even starting to show. The rebuild eliminates this entirely.
✓ Fixed in the rebuildZero total blocking time. No third-party scripts loading unnecessarily. The page responds instantly to every tap.
♿
Three accessibility issues — including text that's hard to read and iframes without labels
Accessibility 90 · contrast issues · iframe without title · ARIA role misuse
For a firm that explicitly serves older clients and offers home visits for people who struggle to travel, accessibility matters particularly. Google found three issues: some text on the site doesn't have enough contrast between the foreground and background colour to be easily readable (which disproportionately affects older visitors or those with visual impairments), an iframe without a title label (affecting screen readers), and an ARIA role applied to an incompatible element. None of these are serious, but they add up to a score of 90 when 98 is achievable — and they signal to Google that the site isn't fully polished.
✓ Fixed in the rebuildFull WCAG 2.1 AA compliance. All contrast ratios meet the standard. All iframes labelled. ARIA roles used correctly. Accessibility 98.
🤖
AI tools and voice assistants can only half-navigate the site
Agentic Browsing 1/2 — one of two checks failing
Google's Agentic Browsing test checks whether AI tools — like Google's AI Overview, ChatGPT or voice assistants — can properly read and navigate your site. The current site scores 1 out of 2. For a firm that serves older clients who may increasingly be asking their smart speaker or phone's assistant to "find me a wills solicitor in Sheffield", being properly readable by those tools is becoming more important. The current score means the site is partially visible to AI tools, but not fully — so you may be missing out on that growing slice of how people find legal services.
✓ Fixed in the rebuild2/2 on Agentic Browsing. Properly structured for AI tools, voice assistants and search engine crawlers.
