What's Going Wrong

Here's what Google found — in plain English, with what each issue means for your patients and your practice.

🖼️
Accessibility & SEO · Images
Images have no alt text — invisible to Google and screen readers

What's happening

Multiple images on your site are missing "alt text" — a short description that tells Google (and assistive technology like screen readers) what the image shows. When alt text is missing, Google can't understand what the image is, and anyone using a screen reader just hears "image" with no context.

What this means for your clients

For anyone visiting your site with visual impairment who uses a screen reader — they lose context for a significant part of your content. And for Google, it's a missed opportunity: images with good alt text can appear in Google Image search, bringing in additional visitors who might then book an appointment.

The fix

Every image on a rebuilt site gets descriptive, search-relevant alt text written in as standard. Not a big change, but genuinely meaningful for both accessibility and SEO.

🔍
SEO · Visibility
No meta description — Google fills in the gap badly

What's happening

Your site is missing a meta description — the snippet of text that appears under your link in Google search results. Without one, Google either shows nothing, or pulls a random sentence from the page, which rarely makes a compelling case for clicking through to you.

What this means for your clients

Even with your SEO score of 85, new clients searching "independent vets Sheffield," "vets Lightwood Lane" or "emergency vet S8" may see your listing but not click it — because the snippet doesn't tell them what makes you special. For an independent practice competing against corporate chains that have marketing teams, every click matters.

The fix

A rebuilt site includes a carefully written meta description — tuned to highlight what makes Green Vets different: independent, 24/7 emergency line, located in Sheffield S8.

📋
Accessibility · Forms
Your contact form isn't labelled properly

What's happening

The form fields on your contact/enquiry form don't have properly associated labels. In plain English: the form works visually, but if someone is using a screen reader or keyboard navigation — they can't tell what each field is for. The technology reads "text input" rather than "your name" or "email address."

What this means for your clients

For clients with visual impairment who use screen readers, filling in your contact form becomes a frustrating guessing game. Some will give up. That's an enquiry — and potentially a long-term client — lost before they've even got in touch.

The fix

A rebuilt site has all form fields properly labelled — both visually and in the underlying code. The form is easy to use for everyone, on any device.

📏
Accessibility · Structure
Heading structure skips levels — confuses screen readers

What's happening

Your page headings (H1, H2, H3 etc.) don't follow a logical sequence — they jump around, skipping levels. In plain English: it's a bit like a document that goes from Chapter 1 straight to Subsection 1.3.2 without the middle sections. Screen readers use heading structure to navigate pages — when it's irregular, the experience breaks down.

What this means for your clients

Clients using assistive technology to navigate your site can't easily jump between sections or understand the structure of the page. It also contributes slightly to a lower accessibility score, which Google takes into account as a quality signal.

The fix

A rebuild uses a clean, logical heading hierarchy from the start — every section labelled correctly, easy to navigate for everyone.

🐌
Performance · Load Speed
7.1 second load time in lab tests — even if real users are faster

What's happening

Google's lab test measured a Largest Contentful Paint (the time until the main visible content appears) of 7.1 seconds. The good news is that your real-world Core Web Vitals data — measured from actual Chrome users — shows faster results, meaning the experience in practice is better than the lab suggests. But there's still a meaningful gap to close.

What this means for your clients

Most of your clients are probably loading the site quickly enough — the real-user data passing is a good sign. But some users, particularly on slower connections or older phones, may still experience slow loads. A performance score of 72 also has some effect on Google's search ranking algorithm, and there are easy wins available (1,350ms savings from render-blocking resources, 442 KB savings from image optimisation).

The fix

A bespoke rebuild eliminates the render-blocking resources, properly compresses and sizes every image, and removes the platform overhead from the current website builder. Target: performance 97+ on mobile.

🏗️
Platform
Built on MyWebsite NOW — a platform with performance ceilings

What's happening

Your site is built on a platform called MyWebsite NOW (a white-label Ionos product). Like most website builders, it loads a lot of code that isn't specific to your site — contributing to the render-blocking issues and some of the performance overhead that Google flags.

What this means for you

The accessibility issues — missing alt text, form labels, heading structure — are harder to fix properly in a page builder than in a bespoke build. You're also paying a monthly subscription for a platform that's working against your scores. A custom site is faster, more flexible, and owned by you outright.

The fix

A clean bespoke rebuild — no platform, no monthly page-builder subscription, no code you don't need. Lightweight, fast, fully accessible from the start.
